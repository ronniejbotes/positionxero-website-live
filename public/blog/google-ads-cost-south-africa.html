How Much Do Google Ads Cost in South Africa, in Rands?

What you pay Google, what you pay SARS, what you pay an agency, and how to work out your own numbers instead of trusting anyone's table.

The Short Answer

You pay Google per click. South African agencies put the all-industry search average at R9 to R15, with legal, insurance and finance far higher, but none of them publishes a sample size and their sector tables contradict each other badly. Add 15% VAT, recoverable only if you are VAT registered. Add management, quoted locally between R1,500 and R20,000 a month, or 10 to 20% of spend at some agencies. Google enforces no minimum on Search.

Search for what Google Ads cost in South Africa and you get agency blogs with rand tables that look authoritative. Read two and they disagree. One puts home services at R3 to R15 a click, another at R15 to R40. Neither says where the number came from.

This article gives you those ranges anyway, because they are the only published South African figures that exist, but it shows the spread instead of hiding it. Then it covers the three cost facts the local pages skip: VAT, permanent billing currency, and what Google actually enforces as a minimum. For the service version, see our Google Ads South Africa page.

A Budget With Three Parts

A South African Google Ads budget has three parts, and most local quotes blend at least two. Ad spend goes to Google and buys clicks. VAT goes to SARS, collected by Google. Management goes to whoever runs the account.

A quote of "R10,000 a month" means nothing until you know which of the three it covers. Our own paid ads service bills management separately from ad spend, worth insisting on whoever you hire: it stops a fee increase hiding inside a spend increase.

How South African Clicks Compare With American Ones

The US side of this is actual data. LocalIQ's 2026 search advertising benchmarks, last updated 1 June 2026, put the average cost per click across all industries at $5.42 and the average cost per lead at $66.69. The page states no geography, but the advertiser base is American.

At R16.00 to the dollar, where Trading Economics had USD/ZAR on 8 September 2026, that $5.42 is about R87. Against local estimates of R9 to R15, a South African click looks six to ten times cheaper. Legal closes the gap most: LocalIQ's $9.87 for Attorneys and Legal Services is roughly R158, inside the R45 to R200 spread the SA agencies quote for that sector.

An auction price is set by how many advertisers bid on the same keyword in the same place. The IAB and PwC Internet Advertising Revenue Report, released 16 April 2026, put US search advertising revenue at $114.2 billion for full year 2025. Research and Markets, published 10 February 2026, forecasts South African digital ad spend of $2.40 billion for 2026. Different measures, so read order of magnitude rather than a ratio.

A thin auction cuts both ways, and that part gets skipped. The same shortage of bidders that keeps South African clicks cheap comes with smaller search volumes, so a cheap click does not automatically produce a cheap lead.

VAT: The 15% Almost Nobody Budgets For

Google Ads Help states that all Google Ads accounts in South Africa are subject to VAT at the South African rate, and have been since 1 April 2019. SARS puts the standard rate at 15%.

How it hits depends on how you pay. Google's documentation says postpay adds VAT to your costs at the end of every month, while prepay subtracts it from your balance before the balance is depleted. Prepay makes it obvious. Put R100 into a prepay South African account and R15 of that is VAT, so R85 buys clicks.

If you are a VAT-registered vendor, that 15% is input tax and you claim it back against a valid tax invoice. If you are not registered, every estimate in the table above is 15% higher for you than it looks.

That distinction just got bigger. SARS raised the compulsory VAT registration threshold from R1 million to R2.3 million on 1 April 2026, and the voluntary threshold from R50,000 to R120,000. A band of small businesses that would previously have had to register now sits outside the net, and for them the 15% never comes back. That is a question for your accountant. Google's own help page says it cannot give tax advice.

Check Your Billing Currency Before Anything Else

Google Ads Help is unambiguous: "Your currency is permanently set when you set up your account. This setting cannot be updated because it is used to determine how you are billed." The options depend on the country you registered in, and the only way to change is a new account.

Open Billing and look. If your account was opened in dollars, every rand budget you set is really a dollar budget with an exchange rate bolted on, and the only fix is a fresh account, losing the history the bidding algorithms learn from.

What Minimum Spend Actually Makes Sense

Google enforces no minimum on Search. The only budget floor it has announced is $5 a day for Demand Gen campaigns from 1 April 2026, reported by Search Engine Land from Google's Ads API announcement, roughly R80 a day at about R16 to the dollar. It does not touch Search.

Google Ads Help defines your daily spending limit as two times your average daily budget for most campaigns, and your monthly limit as 30.4 times it. A R300 daily budget can spend R600 on a busy Tuesday, but your calendar-month ceiling is about R9,120.

The real floor is data. Google's Target CPA documentation recommends measuring performance over the last 30 days including at least 30 conversions. Below that, neither you nor Google's bidding can separate signal from noise.

Run it backwards in rands. At R25 a click and an 8% landing page conversion rate, 30 conversions needs 375 clicks, about R9,400 a month before VAT. In legal at R60 a click, the same 30 conversions needs roughly R22,500. That is the honest answer to "what is the minimum". Your click price and your conversion rate set that floor. It also shows the R3,000 starter budget for what it is: 120 clicks, and maybe ten leads, which is not enough to optimise on.

Work Out Your Own Maximum Cost Per Lead

Every number above is an input. The one that decides whether Google Ads works for you is your maximum acceptable cost per lead: average job value, times gross margin, times close rate. A business with a R6,000 average job, a 45% margin and a 30% close rate makes R2,700 gross profit per job, so each enquiry is worth about R810. Hold marketing to a third of that and your ceiling is roughly R270 a lead.

Now run it forward. At R25 a click and an 8% conversion rate you are paying R312 a lead, before VAT and before any fee. That is over the ceiling. Getting the CPC down means winning an auction against advertisers who want it as much as you do. Lifting the conversion rate to 11% means fixing the page, which is in your control and costs you once instead of monthly. That is why we push landing page work before bid work.

For the deeper arithmetic, how much a lead is worth walks through the value calculation and cost per lead benchmarks by industry gives you something to check the ceiling against. Turning that ceiling into a pipeline is what our South African lead generation work is built around.

Get Your Own Numbers Out of Keyword Planner

None of the ranking cost pages includes this, and it makes every table on the internet unnecessary, including the one above. Open Keyword Planner inside a Google Ads account. Set the location to your actual service area, not South Africa as a whole, because a Sandton bid and a Polokwane bid are different auctions. Check the account currency. Enter the five to ten keywords that produce phone calls, not the two hundred you would like to rank for. Read the two top of page bid columns.

Google's documentation explains them: the low range approximates the 20th percentile advertisers have historically paid for that keyword's top of page bid, the high range the 80th percentile, and bid range statistics cover the last 30 days. Low-volume keywords may show nothing, which is itself useful.

That is your keywords, in your city, priced this month. Do it before you sign anything with anybody, us included.

What Agency Management Costs Here

Published fee ranges spread almost as widely as the CPC estimates, and most of them describe the market rather than the agency doing the publishing. Launch Digital puts management at R1,500 to R7,500 and up, Growth Pulse Media at R3,500 to R8,000 a month plus a R3,000 to R8,000 setup fee, Juicy Designs at R4,000 to R10,000, and Scope at R5,000 to R20,000 and up. Two of them then print their own number, which is more useful than any range: Scope charges a flat R10,000 a month, Juicy Designs from R6,000. Launch Digital and Juicy Designs also offer 10 to 20% of ad spend as an alternative. Scope names that model as common locally and says it does not use one, and Growth Pulse Media quotes no percentage at all.

The percentage model deserves thought. At 15%, a R10,000 budget produces a R1,500 fee, which does not buy serious attention. It also means your agency earns more when your spend rises, a conflict worth naming rather than pretending away.

Our own pricing is public in pricing.md and quoted in dollars, our source-of-truth currency: Google Ads management from $850 a month excluding ad spend, with a $500 minimum monthly ad spend, month to month. At about R16 to the dollar on 8 September 2026 that is approximately R13,600 and R8,000, but those are rough conversions. South African clients get a rand quote at the rate on the day.

Two questions to ask any agency here. Is the fee separate from ad spend, or blended? And whose name is on the account, so that you keep it if you leave?

Two Things Imported US Advice Gets Wrong

Local Services Ads and the Google Guaranteed badge come up constantly in American articles about home-services advertising. Google's own Local Services Ads country page lists Austria, Belgium, Canada, France, Germany, Ireland, Italy, Spain, Switzerland, the United Kingdom and the United States. South Africa is not on it as of September 2026. Check the selector yourself before building a plan around it, because lists change.

US cost-per-lead benchmarks are the second trap. LocalIQ's all-industry $66.69 is about R1,070. Use it to understand what American competitors work with. Your own ceiling comes from your own job value, margin and close rate.

When Google Ads Is the Wrong Answer Here

There are two situations where we would tell you to put the money somewhere else.

If your gross profit per job is R800, the search maths is very hard to make work. At a 30% close rate each lead is worth R240, so your ceiling sits near R80. At R15 a click you would need better than a 19% conversion rate to hit that, before VAT and before any fee. Raising your average job value beats raising your budget.

The second is demand. Google Ads harvests search demand that already exists. It does not create demand. If nobody is typing what you sell, no budget fixes that, and Keyword Planner will tell you inside ten minutes. Paid search also stops the day the card stops, which is the honest argument for putting some budget into SEO in South Africa alongside it.

Where to Start

Do the Keyword Planner exercise first. It costs nothing and turns this article into a sanity check rather than a source of truth. Then work out your maximum cost per lead from your own job value, margin and close rate, and see whether the two numbers meet.
