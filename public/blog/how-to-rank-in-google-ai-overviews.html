How do you rank in Google AI Overviews?

There is no separate way to rank in Google AI Overviews. What Google requires, the Search Console setting that can shut you out, and what the click studies show.

There is no separate way to rank in Google AI Overviews. A page can be cited only if it is indexed and eligible to show in Google Search with a snippet, and only if the site has not been excluded from AI features in Search Console. Google says nothing else is required, and nothing guarantees a place. What you control is the page: a plain answer to the question near the top, in visible text, with the source of every figure named.

The facts below come from Google's own documentation or from independent research you can open yourself, and we say where that research is thin. For ChatGPT, Perplexity and the wider subject, see our guide to what AI SEO is.

What does Google say it takes to rank in AI Overviews?

Start with what Google itself has published. Google's Search Central documentation on AI features, last updated 10 December 2025, says that to be eligible as a supporting link in AI Overviews or AI Mode, "a page must be indexed and eligible to be shown in Google Search with a snippet", and that "There are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode". The same page says you do not need new machine readable files, AI text files or special schema.org structured data.

So the bar is ordinary: indexed, snippets allowed, no stray nosnippet or max-snippet:0. One documented mechanism does matter. Google says both features may use a "query fan-out" technique, running several related searches across subtopics, which lets them show "a wider and more diverse set of helpful links" than a classic web search. So a page can be linked for a related sub-question even when it does not appear in the classic results for the words the searcher typed. Every supporting link must still be an indexed page, which is why ordinary SEO work is still the foundation.

There is now one setting to check as well. Since 31 August 2026, every site in Search Console has a Search generative AI control, under Settings. It includes your site by default. If someone switches it to exclude, Google says links to your site and its content will not appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode.

Can you see your AI Overview impressions in Search Console?

Google launched Search Generative AI performance reports in Search Console on 3 June 2026, first to a subset of websites, and added a note to the announcement: "As of August 31, 2026, we've rolled out these insights to all websites worldwide." The report shows impressions, broken down by page, country, device and date.

It does not show clicks, queries or position. And the impressions are part of what you already had: the Search Console Help page says the report includes data from the Web search type in the ordinary Performance report. You can now see whether you are visible and on which pages. You still cannot see how many clicks those appearances bring. Google Analytics will not separate them either, because it counts visits from AI Overviews and AI Mode as Organic Search, as our guide to tracking AI assistant traffic in Google Analytics 4 explains. Record a baseline anyway.

How many clicks do AI Overviews take away?

Pew Research Center tracked 900 US adults across 68,879 Google searches in March 2025. When an AI summary appeared, they clicked a traditional search result on 8% of visits, against 15% when none appeared.

The strongest evidence is a randomised field experiment by Saharsh Agarwal of the Indian School of Business and Ananya Sen of Carnegie Mellon University. A browser extension hid AI Overviews from a randomly chosen group of Chrome users. In their working paper, an overview cut outbound organic clicks by 39.8% when it appeared, and sponsored clicks did not change. The paper has not yet been published in a journal, so the exact figure may move, but it points the same way as Pew.

Google's own page on AI features says that clicks from results pages with AI Overviews are "higher quality", meaning people are more likely to spend more time on the site. It gives no figures for that. The working paper measured engagement after the click and found no difference between the groups that did and did not see overviews.

Is being cited in an AI Overview worth anything?

Less than you might hope. In the Pew data, people clicked a link inside the AI summary itself on just 1% of visits to pages that showed one. A citation limits the damage an overview does to your traffic. It does not undo it, so be wary of anyone who sells one as pure upside.

What makes a page worth citing?

Google does not publish a list of what it prefers beyond its ordinary advice: helpful, reliable content written for people, with the important content available as text. Within that, two habits are worth building. The first is a short answer directly under a heading that asks the question, near the top of the page. That is our advice rather than a measured finding, and it helps readers either way.

The second is a named source beside every figure. The GEO research paper, accepted to the KDD 2024 conference, tested content changes against a benchmark of 10,000 queries. Adding statistics, quotations from credible sources and citations made pages more visible in AI-written answers, and the authors report gains of up to 40%. Keyword stuffing did little. The tests ran on an engine the authors built and on Perplexity, not on Google's AI Overviews, so treat the result as direction rather than proof.

Do schema markup and llms.txt help?

Not for AI Overviews, as far as Google is concerned. As the page quoted above says, you need no special schema.org structured data and no AI text files, which is the category llms.txt falls into. Its one instruction on structured data is to make sure it matches the visible text on the page. We keep BlogPosting, BreadcrumbList and Person markup on our own articles because it does its job for breadcrumbs and for saying who wrote what. We do not claim it wins citations.

We also publish an llms.txt file, because it costs almost nothing to keep, but we do not treat it as a way into AI Overviews.

What should you do this month?

  1. Record a baseline from the generative AI report in Search Console. It counts appearances by page, not visits, so read it that way.
  2. Confirm eligibility: pages indexed, snippets allowed, no stray nosnippet or max-snippet:0, and the Search generative AI control in Search Console still set to include your site.
  3. Take the buyer questions that matter most to you and put a short direct answer under a heading phrased as each question, near the top of the page.
  4. Put every figure in visible text, with a named source and a date.
  5. Be specific. Write the suburb and the month, not "the area" and "recently".
  6. Leave new files and special markup off the list. Google says you do not need them, so put that time into the pages themselves.

Can anyone guarantee you a place in AI Overviews?

No. Google says that even a page meeting every requirement is not guaranteed to be crawled, indexed or shown. Google alone picks the links beside each answer, so nobody outside Google can promise you a citation, us included.

What can be done is the groundwork: keep your pages indexed and eligible, rewrite the pages that answer your buyers' real questions so the answer comes first, source every number, and earn links and mentions on other reputable sites. That is what our SEO work covers.
