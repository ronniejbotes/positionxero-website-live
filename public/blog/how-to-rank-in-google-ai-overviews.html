How to Get Your Business Cited in Google AI Overviews

Every number below carries its sample size, its date and its caveat. Several of them say less than the guides quoting them claim.

How Do You Get Cited in Google AI Overviews?

To be cited in an AI Overview, a page must be indexed and eligible to be shown in Google Search with a snippet. Google says there are no additional technical requirements. Past that bar, what correlates with citation is answering the exact question in one self-contained passage near the top of the page, in visible text, with named sources and precise entity names.

Most people arrive at this question from the same place: rankings held, traffic did not, and there is a grey box sitting above the listing. Here is the answer, with the evidence attached and the gaps admitted. Our guide to what AI SEO is covers the groundwork.

Google's Documented Requirement Is Two Conditions

Start with the primary source, because most guides skip it. Google's Search Central documentation on AI features, updated 10 December 2025, states: "To be eligible to be shown as a supporting link in AI Overviews or AI Mode, a page must be indexed and eligible to be shown in Google Search with a snippet, fulfilling the Search technical requirements. There are no additional technical requirements." It also says: "You don't need to create new machine readable files, AI text files, or markup to appear in these features. There's also no special schema.org structured data that you need to add."

So the bar is boring: indexed, snippet allowed, no stray nosnippet or max-snippet:0. Everything past that is observed correlation, not documented requirement. One documented mechanism does matter. Both features may use a "query fan-out" technique, issuing multiple related searches across subtopics, so you compete for sub-questions you will never see. That is why ordinary SEO work is the substrate: fan-out retrieves from the index.

Search Console Now Shows AI Overview Impressions, and Still Hides the Rest

Google launched a Generative AI performance report in Search Console on 3 June 2026, first to a subset of UK site owners, as Barry Schwartz reported at Search Engine Land. It later told Search Engine Journal: "As of August 31, 2026, we've rolled out these insights to all websites worldwide."

Google's Search Console Help documentation lists impressions grouped by Pages, Countries, Dates and Devices. No clicks, no click-through rate, no position, no queries. Nor is it new inventory: the same Help page says those impressions were already counted inside the "Web" search type of the ordinary Performance report.

You can now answer "am I visible, and on which pages", and still not answer "is it earning me anything". Take a baseline and treat it as a floor. The same rollout added a toggle to block your content from AI features, and Google says sites that opt out receive no traffic or impressions from them. For a business chasing leads, that trade rarely makes sense.

How Big the Click Loss Is, Study by Study

Three studies get quoted interchangeably and measure different things. Pew Research Center tracked 900 US adults across 68,879 Google searches in March 2025: on pages carrying an AI summary, users clicked a traditional result on 8% of visits against 15% without one. Ahrefs, measuring position-one desktop click-through rate across 300,000 keywords, compared December 2023 with December 2025 and published a 58% drop in February 2026. It calls that correlation, not causation.

Then there is the one study with a control group. Saharsh Agarwal of the Indian School of Business and Ananya Sen of Carnegie Mellon's Heinz College ran a randomised field experiment with a Chrome extension that hid AI Overviews from a randomly assigned group: 1,065 US desktop Chrome users, 68,089 searches, early 2026. Outbound organic clicks fell 39.8% when an overview was shown, 0.62 per search hidden against 0.37 visible, and zero-click probability rose from 0.54 to 0.73. Sponsored clicks did not move.

Google's explanation is that the lost clicks were low-value "bounce clicks", an argument Liz Reid has made in a Google blog post, a Wall Street Journal interview and on Bloomberg's Odd Lots. Search Engine Journal's Matt G. Southern reviewed all three: "In none of those three appearances has Reid provided supporting data." The randomised experiment found no difference in bounce rate, time on the downstream page or reported satisfaction. Google may still be right. Nobody has shown it.

The Value of a Citation, Measured in Clicks

Seer Interactive published the most useful number here in April 2026, from 53 brands and 2.43 billion organic impressions. Being cited delivers "+120% more organic clicks per impression versus when you are not cited", and still underperforms a result with no AI Overview by 38%. Per million organic impressions on informational queries:

ScenarioOrganic clicks per million impressions
No AI Overview present~33,500
AI Overview present, brand cited~20,743
AI Overview present, brand not cited~9,445

Seer's own caveat: "We cannot claim causation. Higher-authority brands are also more likely to be cited." Winning the citation roughly doubles your clicks against losing it and still leaves you short of the row above. Winning the citation is damage limitation. An agency selling it as upside has deleted that top row.

Why Ranking Page One Stopped Being Enough

Ahrefs analysed 863,000 keyword SERPs and 4 million AI Overview URLs in March 2026. Only 37.9% of cited URLs appeared in the first ten blocks of the SERP, and 31.0% ranked nowhere in the top 100. Its July 2025 study had the top-10 share near 76%. Ahrefs reads the swing as fan-out pulling more sources from sub-query SERPs, and it also improved its citation parsing between runs, so take 76% to 38% as direction rather than an exact delta.

Semrush's July 2025 study of 5,000 random keywords put the same overlap near 67%. Different keyword sets, different parsers, both reputable, and neither figure is a fact about Google. Top-ten rankings still help, and are no longer close to sufficient.

Five Things That Correlate With Being Cited

  • The answer near the top, in one self-contained passage. Kevin Indig analysed 3 million ChatGPT responses and 30 million citations, reported by Danny Goodwin at Search Engine Land in February 2026: 44.2% of citations came from the first 30% of a page, and cited passages were nearly twice as likely to use a clear definition. Adam Gnuse's November 2025 audit of 15 domains found 72.4% of cited posts carried an "answer capsule" of 120 to 150 characters under a question-shaped heading. Both study ChatGPT, not AI Overviews.
  • Naming things precisely. In Indig's data, heavily cited text averaged 20.6% proper nouns against a typical 5 to 8% for English. Write "Dallas–Fort Worth", "September 2026" and the model number instead of "the area", "recently" and "the unit".
  • Figures with a named source. The GEO paper at ACM SIGKDD 2024 tested nine content changes across a 10,000-query benchmark. Cited sources, quotations and statistics led the field at a 30 to 40% relative improvement on its main visibility metric, while keyword stuffing performed poorly. It predates AI Overviews.
  • Being mentioned off your own site. Ahrefs ran Spearman correlations across 75,000 brands in May 2025: branded web mentions 0.664, Domain Rating 0.326, backlinks 0.218, with its own "correlation ≠ causation" warning and every factor in the weak-to-moderate band.
  • Reasonable freshness, not date fiddling. Across 16.975 million cited URLs in July 2025, Ahrefs found AI-cited content averaged 1,064 days old against 1,432 for organic results, and AI Overviews cited the oldest content of any surface measured. Those numbers are over a year old now, so trust the ordering more than the day counts. Bumping a publish date without changing the page is not a strategy.

Two Things That Do Not Work

Schema markup as an AI Overview lever. Ahrefs tracked 1,885 pages that added JSON-LD, matched each to controls with similar prior citation levels, and ran a difference-in-differences. AI Overview citations fell 4.6% against those controls, a small but statistically significant decline, with AI Mode and ChatGPT indistinguishable from zero. Every page studied was already cited heavily, the window was 30 days, and Ahrefs calls the decline "real but unexplained".

We ship Organization and BreadcrumbList markup across this site, plus BlogPosting and Person on every article, and we are not removing any of it, because it earns its place on breadcrumbs and entity resolution. What changes is the claim. A searchVIU test in October 2025 asked five AI systems to read a price that existed only in JSON-LD. None of them found it.

llms.txt. Google's documentation already says you do not need AI text files. Ahrefs checked 137,210 domains with traffic in May 2026: 28% published one, 97% of those files received zero requests that month, and AI retrieval bots accounted for 1.1% of the requests that did arrive. We publish one anyway and explain why in our AI SEO guide. Zero downside is not the same as a priority.

Being Cited Is Not the Same as Being Recommended

Seer Interactive's John Lovett analysed 541,213 LLM responses across 20 brands and six platforms in February 2026. When a brand was named in the answer, its citation rate was 53.1%. When it was not, 10.6%. Seer calls the second case a ghost citation: "when your content is trusted enough to be sourced, but your brand isn't known well enough to be mentioned."

Their reading is that the model picks brands from what it already knows and then goes looking for sources: "the citations are the bibliography, not the brainstorm". They add, to their credit, that they cannot see inside the model, so this is "strongly supported behavioural evidence, not proven architecture". The uncomfortable consequence: your page can be the evidence in an answer recommending a competitor.

If You Sell to a City, Read This Before You Panic

Whitespark's Miriam Ellis ran 540 local queries across Houston, Phoenix and Denver in six industries in May 2025, by intent. Local-intent queries showed an AI Overview 15% of the time and a local pack 93%. Informational queries: 92% and 6%. She calls it a modest-sized study and it is over a year old, so take the direction, not the decimals. "Emergency plumber Fort Worth" still returns a map pack; "how much does it cost to replace a water heater" returns an overview.

BrightLocal's March 2026 survey of 1,002 US adults found 45% had used AI for local recommendations in the previous year, against 6% a year earlier, and 97% of those AI users say they at least sometimes double-check against real reviews, 42% of them always. Reviews and the local pack decide the hire-me query. The AI answer decides the research question ahead of it, which our guide to local SEO for service businesses covers.

Is It Different for ChatGPT and Perplexity?

The gap is wider than most assume. Ahrefs ran 15,000 long-tail queries in July 2025, then posed the same prompts to four assistants and checked which cited URLs also appeared in Google's top 10. ChatGPT, Gemini and Copilot each landed near 8%. Perplexity was the outlier at 28.6%, which pulls the four-assistant average to 12%.

The one genuinely distinct action is crawler access. OpenAI documents OAI-SearchBot as the crawler behind ChatGPT's search features and says sites disallowing it "will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers". Perplexity documents PerplexityBot the same way and recommends allowing it. Check both in your robots.txt. Beyond that, the work is the same work.

Expect the ground to move. seoClarity tracked ChatGPT citations across five markets in 2026: after 8 March the US zero-citation rate doubled from 28% to 48%, and after 19 April volumes fell 86 to 94% before recovering toward pre-March levels in May. Nothing a site owner did caused that. We build AI assistants and agents at Cognexa, which is useful as calibration: citation is a ranking decision with a model on top, and it changes when the model does.

Six Things Worth Doing This Month

  1. Record a baseline from the Generative AI performance report in Search Console. Impressions only, by page, so it answers visibility, not value.
  2. Confirm the eligibility conditions: pages indexed, snippets allowed, no stray nosnippet or max-snippet:0, and robots.txt allowing OAI-SearchBot and PerplexityBot. Our free audit covers the indexing and snippet side.
  3. Take your ten highest-value buyer questions and put a 20 to 25 word answer directly under a heading phrased as that question, in the top third of the page.
  4. Put every figure in visible text with a named source and a date. Never only in JSON-LD.
  5. Replace pronouns with names. "The area" becomes "Plano and Frisco". "Recently" becomes the month.
  6. Spend the llms.txt hours getting mentioned somewhere that is not your own website.

What Nobody Can Honestly Tell You Yet

  • How often AI Overviews appear. Semrush's study of more than 10 million keywords put it at 15.69% of queries in November 2025, against a July peak of 24.61%. BrightEdge told Search Engine Journal in March 2026 that they trigger on nearly half of tracked queries. Both are true of their own keyword sets.
  • There is no free citation-share metric. Search Console reports impressions, not citations, clicks or queries, and the vendor trackers disagree.
  • The causal evidence is two studies: one randomised experiment on 1,065 Chrome users, and one difference-in-differences on pages already cited heavily. Everything else here, including the parts we act on, is correlation.
  • The systems move underneath you. An 86 to 94% citation swing inside three months is not a content problem, and nobody can insulate you from the next one.

If a supplier will not say those four things out loud, you have learned something useful about them.

What We Can and Cannot Do About It

We cannot promise you a citation, and neither can anyone else, because nobody controls the retrieval stage. What is doable follows from the evidence above: get the eligibility conditions right, restructure the pages that answer your buyers' real questions, source every number, and build mentions on sites you do not own. That is what our SEO work covers.
