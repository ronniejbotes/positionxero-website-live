What is AI SEO, and what can a South African business do about it?

AI SEO is the work of making your website easy for AI search tools to find, understand and quote. Those tools are Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode, ChatGPT's search, Perplexity and Microsoft Copilot. When someone asks one of them "who fits geysers in Centurion?" or "what does a website cost in South Africa?", the answer is built from pages it can read. AI SEO is about being one of those pages, with your facts stated clearly enough to be used.

Most of it is ordinary SEO done properly. A few parts are new.

AI SEO, GEO, AEO: one job, several labels

You'll see the same work sold as AI SEO, GEO (generative engine optimisation), AEO (answer engine optimisation) or LLM SEO. Whatever the label, the job is the same: be findable, be readable, say true things plainly. If a supplier tells you their acronym is a different discipline from the others, ask what they'd actually do differently on your site.

Where AI answers come from

Each tool finds pages its own way, so it helps to know what each company says.

Google AI Overviews and AI Mode. Google says a page has to be indexed and eligible to show in Search with a snippet, and that there are no additional requirements or special optimisations beyond that. It also says you don't need special schema or new AI text files.

ChatGPT search. OpenAI's crawler for search is OAI-SearchBot, and OpenAI recommends allowing it in your robots.txt if you want to appear in ChatGPT's search results. Its other crawler, GPTBot, collects content that may be used to train its models. They're separate, so you can decide on each one.

Perplexity. PerplexityBot is the crawler that surfaces and links websites in Perplexity's results. Perplexity asks you to allow it.

Microsoft Copilot and Bing. Bing Webmaster Tools has an AI Performance report that shows how often your pages are cited in Copilot, in Bing's AI-generated summaries and in some partner tools.

So the first two jobs are plain: get indexed in Google and Bing, and make sure the AI search crawlers aren't blocked.

Six things to do, in order

  1. Check you're indexed. Set up Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools, submit your sitemap, and look for pages that aren't indexed. A page no search engine has can't be in an AI answer.
  2. Let the AI search crawlers in. Open yoursite.co.za/robots.txt and look for any rule that blocks OAI-SearchBot or PerplexityBot. Check your host's or CDN's bot-blocking settings too, since those can block crawlers without touching robots.txt.
  3. Say your facts in words. What you do, where you do it, what it costs and how to reach you, in sentences on the page. A price in rand in the text is readable. A price inside an image isn't.
  4. Make each section answer its own heading. Put the answer in the first sentence under the heading, and write the section so it still makes sense if someone reads only that part. Whatever gets quoted then still makes sense on its own.
  5. Add structured data that matches the page. Google asks that structured data match the visible text. It describes your business; it doesn't replace what's written on the page.
  6. Keep your details the same everywhere. Your business name, number and website should read the same on your site, your Google Business Profile and every directory you're listed in, so nobody, person or machine, has to guess which version is right.

Does llms.txt do anything?

llms.txt is a proposal from September 2024 for a plain-text file at the root of your site that summarises your business for AI tools, the way robots.txt and a sitemap help crawlers. Google says its AI features don't need new AI text files.

We publish one on our own site and add one to the sites we build, for a narrow reason: it takes minutes, it's an accurate summary anyone can read, and it costs nothing. We don't sell it as a service, and you shouldn't pay anyone to maintain one.

How to measure it

There's no ranking report for AI answers, but there are more ways to measure it than there used to be.

  • Search Console's generative AI report. Since 31 August 2026, every site has had a report of impressions in Google's generative AI features, by page, country, device and date. It shows impressions, not clicks.
  • Bing's AI Performance report. Citations in Copilot and Bing's AI summaries, which pages were cited and a sample of the searches behind them.
  • Visits from AI assistants in Google Analytics. Treat it as a minimum. Our guide to tracking ChatGPT traffic in Google Analytics 4 shows how, and what it misses.
  • Your own spot checks. Write down a dozen questions your customers would ask, put them to ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google once a month, and note whether you're mentioned. It's a small sample, so use it to spot changes and never set a target on it.

If someone sells you a "citation rate", ask how they measured it.

Mistakes to avoid

Adding markup instead of answers. If the answer is only in your structured data and not on the page, you've described a page that doesn't exist.

Burying the answer. A definition in the sixth paragraph is hard to lift. Put it under the heading that asks the question.

Numbers with no source. A statistic nobody can trace is a reason not to quote you. Link the source in the same sentence.

Blocking the crawlers by accident. A security setting can turn away a crawler you wanted. Check after any change to your hosting.

Paying for a guarantee. No supplier controls what Google, OpenAI, Perplexity or Microsoft show. Google's guide to hiring an SEO says no one can guarantee a number one ranking, and the same goes for an AI mention.

What it looks like for a South African small business

Local detail is your advantage. A national content site can't write your prices in rand, the suburbs you really cover, or what happens on a job in your area, and those are the specifics an AI answer needs when someone asks a local question. So put them in words on the page: the suburbs you cover, what a standard job costs in rand, and how soon you can get there. A sentence like that does more for you than a contact form and a stock photo.

Pair that with a Google Business Profile, if your business qualifies, and the same name and number everywhere you're listed.

Questions people ask

What is AI SEO?

AI SEO is making your website easy for AI search tools like Google's AI Overviews, ChatGPT and Perplexity to find, understand and quote. Most of it is good SEO, plus making sure the AI search crawlers can read your site.

Is AI SEO the same as GEO or AEO?

In practice, yes. GEO, AEO, LLM SEO and AI SEO describe the same job: be findable, be readable and state your facts plainly. Treat the choice of label as branding.

Should I block GPTBot?

That's your call. OpenAI says GPTBot collects content that may be used to train its models, while OAI-SearchBot is what puts sites in ChatGPT's search results. They're separate crawlers, so you can block one and allow the other.

How long does AI SEO take to work?

Nobody can promise a date. Google's SEO starter guide says some changes take effect in a few hours and others take several months, and no one controls what an AI tool decides to quote.

Does llms.txt improve AI visibility?

Google says its AI features don't need new AI text files. We publish an accurate llms.txt anyway because it's cheap and harmless, but it shouldn't be sold as a service.
