The SA Advantage

How click-to-WhatsApp ads work

WhatsApp is the default business channel in South Africa in a way it isn't in the US or UK, and your campaigns should reflect that.

A click-to-WhatsApp ad replaces the usual "Learn More" link with a button that opens a WhatsApp chat with your business number, pre-filled with a message you write. The prospect stays in an app they already have open. There's no form to fill in and no page to wait for on a patchy mobile connection, which removes most of the reasons a good enquiry disappears between the ad and the first conversation.

Customers here already message plumbers, PV installers, borehole drillers, armed response companies and estate agents on WhatsApp, so a lead form asks them to do something they wouldn't normally do. A chat also gives you something a lead form never will: a written record of what the person wants, in their own words, before you've spent a minute on the phone.

01

Write the pre-filled message

The pre-filled text is your first qualification filter. "Hi, I would like a quote for a 5kW inverter and battery backup in Centurion" self-selects a buyer. "Hi, I am interested" self-selects a browser.

02

Build around one offer

One audience, one problem, one offer per ad set. Mixed messages inside a campaign make the results unreadable and stall the learning phase for no benefit.

03

Set the right conversion event

Optimise for conversations started, and connect the WhatsApp Business Platform so Meta can learn from real message events.

04

Fix the response window

Speed of first reply is the biggest lever. We agree a response target, script the opening replies, and make sure someone owns that inbox during ad hours.

05

Track through to the real outcome

A chat isn't revenue yet. We tag each conversation through to a booked site visit, signed contract or completed install, then feed that back so the algorithm chases buyers instead of chatters.

06

Cut what does not convert

Creative fatigues fast on Meta. We run a rolling test queue and retire ads when the numbers say so, however attached anyone is to them.

Creative

Creative that works in the South African market

Meta rewards whoever holds attention, and here that's rarely the most polished ad in the auction.

Put the rand figure on screen

Showing a price is a strong filter. "From R2,850 fitted" loses you the browsers and keeps the buyers, which is the right trade when every conversation costs money to start.

Sell on keeping the lights on through load-shedding

Backup power, inverters, boreholes and water tanks sell on continuity. Footage of a house with lights on during an outage outperforms a product render every time.

Film it on a phone, on site

Keep it vertical, subtitled, short and filmed on site. A studio production looks like an advert and gets scrolled past. A technician explaining a job looks like a person, and people watch it.

Use faces and places your audience knows

A Durban audience responds to a Durban street. Naming the suburb in the first line of copy lifts relevance far more cheaply than narrowing the targeting radius does.

Show the before and after

Roof repairs, CCTV installs, paving, salon work: the pictures make the case. Two images and one honest caption usually beat a paragraph of claims.

Keep new ads coming

Creative fatigue is the main cost driver on Meta. A steady supply of new angles keeps frequency down and cost per conversation stable, because even a perfect ad wears out.

Compliance

POPIA, consent and WhatsApp leads

Consent is the part that gets expensive when it's ignored.

What POPIA requires of advertisers. The Protection of Personal Information Act governs how South African businesses collect, store and use personal information. For direct marketing by electronic means, consent is required from anyone who is not already your customer, and you may only ask for that consent once. Every data subject also has the right to know what you hold about them, to have it corrected, and to object to its use.

Click-to-WhatsApp has a built-in advantage here. When a prospect taps your ad and opens the chat themselves, they've started the contact with your business. That's a completely different position from cold-messaging a purchased list. The format doesn't make you compliant on its own, but it starts you on the right side of the line.

The practical rules we build into every campaign:

  • Identify the business by name in the first reply, so nobody wonders who is messaging them.
  • Record consent with a timestamp and the source campaign, so you can evidence it later.
  • Never move a WhatsApp enquiry into a broadcast list without a separate, explicit opt-in.
  • Set a retention period for transcripts and keep them access-controlled, not on a personal handset.
  • Publish a privacy notice and link it from every ad destination.
  • Honour an opt-out the moment it arrives, and log that too.

We're marketers, not attorneys, so none of this is legal advice. What we can do is design the capture so consent and record-keeping happen at the point of enquiry, instead of being bolted on after an information officer asks a question you can't answer.

Channel Choice

When Meta beats Google
for a South African business

And when it doesn't, which matters just as much: getting this wrong is the most expensive mistake you can make here.

Start with Meta when…

  • Nobody is searching for your category yet: backup power upgrades, a security offer for a new estate, a clinic launch.
  • The product photographs well and a before-and-after does the persuading.
  • You need volume quickly and can answer WhatsApp within minutes.
  • The decision is emotional as much as practical, at a mid-range ticket.
  • You want a retargeting pool you can sell to for months afterwards.

Start with Google when…

  • Demand already exists and is urgent: a burst geyser, a borehole pump failure, an emergency locksmith, a blocked drain.
  • People search by name for what you sell, such as "solar installers Sandton" or "armed response Pretoria East".
  • You sell high-value B2B where the buyer researches before they enquire.
  • Your close rate on inbound calls is already strong and you just need more of them.

If your monthly budget is small, pick one channel and fund it properly. Our breakdown of Google Ads vs Meta Ads walks through the decision in detail, how much Google Ads cost covers the budget question, and our Google Ads South Africa page handles the search side in rand.
