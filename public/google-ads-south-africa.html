Google Ads Management in South Africa
That Turns High-Intent Search Into Booked Jobs.

Paying for clicks that don't turn into calls? Run from South Africa, for South African businesses.

Budgets planned in rand, our management fee billed separately from your ad spend, and month-to-month terms. Last updated: October 2026. Figures checked October 2026.

What is Google Ads management? Google Ads management in South Africa is the same job as anywhere else. It's easy to describe and hard to do well: someone works out the searches your buyers type, builds the campaigns and ad copy that answer them, sets the bids and budgets, and then spends every week cutting the searches that waste money and putting more behind the ones that bring in work. The management fee pays for that ongoing decision-making. Your ad spend is a separate cost, and in South Africa the two should always appear as separate lines on your invoice.

Why Search

Why high-intent search beats social
for South African service businesses

Paid social interrupts people. Search reaches them at the exact moment they've decided to spend money.

When a geyser bursts in Randburg at 06:00, nobody scrolls Instagram for inspiration. They type "emergency plumber near me" and phone the first result that looks credible. That's the whole case for search. It's also why everything on this page is counted in booked jobs, signed contracts, installs and monitored contracts. A Google search campaign is the only channel that lets you buy that moment directly, once the person has decided.

The downside is that search is capped by demand. Only so many people search "borehole drilling Pretoria" in a month, and once you've captured that demand, your growth has to come from somewhere else. That's where paid social, and later organic SEO, take over. Channels that create demand are how you sell solar to someone who hasn't decided to buy solar yet. For a South African service business we usually start with search, because it pays back fastest and shows you what your buyers want, then add paid social and systematic lead generation once the search account is producing consistently. If you're weighing the two right now, our breakdown of Google Ads vs Meta Ads works through the decision channel by channel.

We know a suburb name often converts better than a city name, and that a quote in rands with VAT stated up front removes an objection before it's raised. Budget Planning

What a realistic minimum
ad spend looks like in rands

Cost per lead isn't a number you get quoted. You work it out yourself, from two inputs you control.

Cost per lead is your cost per click divided by your landing page conversion rate. Anyone who quotes you a guaranteed cost per lead before seeing your site is guessing at one of those two numbers. The table below is plain arithmetic, so find your own row.

Cost per click At 5% conversion At 10% conversion At 15% conversion
R10 R200 per lead R100 per lead R67 per lead
R20 R400 per lead R200 per lead R133 per lead
R35 R700 per lead R350 per lead R233 per lead
R50 R1,000 per lead R500 per lead R333 per lead

The table shows two things. Double your conversion rate and your cost per lead halves, without changing a single bid. That's why the landing page you send paid traffic to usually matters more than the campaign itself, and why we'd rather fix a page than raise a budget. At R25 a click, a R3,000 month doesn't buy enough data to work with: that's 120 clicks, and at a 10% conversion rate that's twelve leads for the whole month, split between several keywords. When Google recommends a Target CPA, it suggests measuring performance over the last 30 days with at least 30 conversions. Twelve is a long way short.

For a competitive industry in Johannesburg or Cape Town, plan an ad budget that buys enough clicks to learn from, and spend it on a handful of high-intent keywords. In a less crowded market (a single suburb, or a niche trade in Gqeberha or Centurion), a smaller budget can work, because fewer advertisers are bidding against you. If your close rate and job value make a metro budget look uncomfortable, do the sums first: our guide to cost per lead benchmarks shows how to work back from what a customer is worth to a budget you can defend.

Uniquely South African

Budgeting Google Ads around the
load-shedding demand spike

A grid announcement can reshape your search demand, so plan for it.

When a stage escalation is announced, searches for inverters, batteries, solar installation, generators, borehole pumps and backup water systems spike. A daily budget cap doesn't know today is different. Your campaign spends its budget by mid-morning and stops showing for the rest of the day, on the very day buyers were ready to sign, while the accounts that planned for it are still showing ads at 16:00. This is how we handle it.

01

Hold a reserve budget line

We agree a monthly baseline plus a reserve you authorise us to release on spike days. It sits unspent most of the month and earns its keep on the days demand spikes.

02

Use seasonal adjustments

Smart Bidding learns from history, and a stage escalation is not in the history. Seasonal adjustments tell the algorithm in advance to expect a higher conversion rate for a defined window.

03

Watch lost impression share

Search lost impression share due to budget is the metric that proves you were capped. If it climbs on a spike day, it was your budget that ran out while the demand was still there.

It works in reverse too. Long quiet stretches with a stable grid pull solar and backup-power demand down hard. That's the time to stop defending a collapsing cost per lead in a category nobody is shopping for this week, and move spend to the categories people buy all year (armed response and CCTV monitoring contracts, water security, maintenance and service plans). Recurring-revenue categories such as monitored security contracts are some of the most stable search demand in the country, because they don't depend on the weather. If you sell solar, our breakdown of what solar leads cost covers the economics of that swing in more detail.

Measurement

Conversion tracking and call tracking,
set up properly

The most common fault we find in South African Google Ads accounts is poor tracking: nothing is measured correctly, so every optimisation decision is a guess.

Conversion actions that mean something

We define conversions as the things you get paid for: a submitted quote request, a booked site visit, a phone call over 60 seconds. A pageview doesn't count. Count vanity conversions and Smart Bidding learns to buy you more of the wrong thing.

Call tracking, because SA buyers phone

South African service buyers call far more than they fill in forms. Google forwarding numbers and dynamic number insertion tie a phone call back to the keyword that caused it, so calls show up in your reporting.

WhatsApp as a conversion path

WhatsApp is the default way to message a business here. A click-to-chat link fires a tracked conversion the same way a form does, and for many trades it will beat the form outright. Leave it out and your reports understate how well the account is doing.

Offline conversion import

A lead isn't revenue yet. Import which leads became signed contracts and bidding can optimise for buyers. It's the single biggest upgrade most accounts have never made.

Consent Mode configuration

Consent Mode v2 adjusts tag behaviour based on what a visitor agreed to. Configured properly, you keep modelled measurement without firing tags you have no lawful basis to fire.

Search terms, weekly

The search terms report shows what people typed, as opposed to what you bid on. Adding negative keywords every week is routine work, and it's where a large share of wasted rand is recovered.

Compliance

POPIA-compliant lead forms

Getting these forms right protects the asset you're paying to build.

What POPIA means for a lead form. The Protection of Personal Information Act requires that you collect personal information for a specified, lawful purpose, that you tell the person what that purpose is, that you keep only what you need for only as long as you need it, and that you can honour their rights to access, correct, object and delete. Section 69 additionally restricts unsolicited electronic direct marketing to people who have consented or who are existing customers being marketed similar products.

On a Google Ads landing page, that comes down to a short list. Ask for the fewest fields that let you quote. Every extra field costs you conversions anyway, so here compliance and performance want the same thing. State the purpose in plain language directly beside the submit button rather than burying it in a policy nobody opens. Link the privacy policy. Use an unticked opt-in checkbox for future marketing, kept separate from the enquiry itself, so consent to be quoted is not silently treated as consent to be mailed forever.

Then keep the proof. Saving the consent state, timestamp and source URL with each lead record is what lets you show you're compliant instead of just saying so. Route data subject requests to a named Information Officer who exists and answers, and set a retention period for dead leads, so you aren't keeping every enquiry since 2019 forever.

It affects your advertising directly too. Customer Match audiences and enhanced conversions both involve uploading customer data to Google, and both need a lawful basis and a privacy notice that covers that use. Get consent right at the form and those tools are there for you later without a scramble. Our guide to what a landing page is and how it converts covers the conversion side of the same page.

Buyer's Guide

Questions to ask before hiring a
PPC agency in Johannesburg or Cape Town

Ask every agency on your shortlist these questions, us included. We'd rather you question us properly than sign up on a good feeling.

"Is the management fee separate from ad spend?"

It should be, and both should be visible. A single blended number makes it impossible to tell whether you are paying for media or for the person managing it, and it hides fee increases inside budget increases.

"Whose name is on the Google Ads account?"

If the agency owns it, your conversion history, audience lists and quality signals leave with them. Insist the account is created under your ownership and that you hold admin access from day one.

"Show me the search terms report."

That's a different report from the keyword list. It shows what people typed and how much went on searches that were never going to convert. If an agency is reluctant to show it, you have your answer.

"What exactly are you counting as a conversion?"

If page views, clicks on a phone number without a duration threshold, or scroll depth are counted, the reported cost per lead is fiction. Ask to see the conversion actions list in the account.

"What is the notice period?"

Month-to-month should be on the table. A twelve-month lock-in with no performance break protects the agency, not you.

"How do you handle POPIA on the lead form?"

If the answer is a blank look, the compliance risk is being transferred to you along with the leads. It's your business that carries it, not the agency's.
